Security at Milli

Last Reviewed · February 18, 2026

Every payout you route through Milli represents money you worked for. Protecting it is our first job. This page summarizes the technical, administrative, and physical safeguards Milli uses to defend your data and describes how to reach us if you believe you have found a vulnerability.

1. Encryption

  • In transit: all connections between the Milli mobile apps, our web dashboard, and our backend are protected with TLS 1.3 with modern ciphers and HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS) preloaded on drivemilli.com.
  • At rest: databases and object storage encrypt customer data at rest using AES-256. Encryption keys are managed by our cloud provider's KMS with automatic rotation.
  • Sensitive fields such as tax identifiers are additionally encrypted at the application layer with keys separated from the primary database.

2. Access controls

  • Access to production systems requires single sign-on with hardware-backed multi-factor authentication. Passwords alone are never sufficient.
  • Employee access follows the principle of least privilege. Access is scoped to job function, granted through a documented request, and reviewed on a recurring cadence.
  • All administrative activity in production is logged to an append-only audit trail retained for a minimum of one year.
  • Customer nonpublic personal information is never copied to employee workstations.

3. Data isolation and tenancy

Each Milli account is scoped by a customer-owned identifier at every layer — API, application logic, and database queries. Cross-tenant access is prevented by row-level authorization checks that are exercised by our test suite on every deployment.

4. Infrastructure and network

  • Milli runs on hardened Linux containers inside a private virtual network. Public ingress is limited to HTTPS through a managed load balancer.
  • Databases are not publicly reachable and require VPN-authenticated access to reach at the operator layer.
  • Third-party dependencies are pinned, scanned for known vulnerabilities on every build, and patched on a defined schedule.

5. Monitoring and detection

We continuously monitor application, network, and identity logs for anomalous behavior — for example, unusual authentication patterns, mass data reads, and privilege escalations. Alerts route to an on-call engineer twenty-four hours a day.

6. Business continuity

  • Encrypted, cross-region backups of customer data are taken on a recurring schedule and periodically test-restored.
  • Documented runbooks cover common failure modes, and we exercise them at least annually.

7. Third-party integrations

Milli connects to your financial institutions through Plaid, bills you through Stripe, and — when activated — routes banking flows through approved federally regulated partners. Each provider is contractually bound to GLBA-equivalent safeguards and is subject to Milli's periodic security review. Milli does not store your online banking credentials at any point; those remain with the aggregator that issued the tokenized connection.

8. Compliance roadmap

Milli's information security program is designed against the AICPA Trust Services Criteria. We are actively working toward a SOC 2 Type II attestation and will publish the report through our Trust Center when it is available. In the meantime, we welcome enterprise security questionnaires — email security@drivemilli.com.

9. Incident response

Milli maintains a written incident response plan that covers detection, containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident review. In the event of a security incident affecting customer nonpublic personal information, we will notify affected customers and, where required, regulators, in accordance with applicable law and without unreasonable delay.

10. Responsible disclosure

If you believe you have discovered a security vulnerability in Milli's app, website, or infrastructure, please report it privately to security@drivemilli.com. We ask that you:

  • Provide enough detail for our team to reproduce the issue.
  • Avoid accessing, modifying, or destroying data belonging to other users.
  • Give us a reasonable window to investigate and remediate before public disclosure.

We will acknowledge your report, keep you informed of progress, and — where the finding is valid and material — credit you publicly if you wish. A formal bug bounty program will be published on this page in the near future.
